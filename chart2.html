    1

    Assess risk in the right group

    ASSESS

    +

    Adults aged 18 and over; not an acute chest-pain pathway – pregnancy/breastfeeding and inherited lipid disorders follow their own guidance. Use QRISK3 for people aged 25–84 without CVD, including type 2 diabetes; NICE removed the public online QRISK3 link (October 2025) as it should not be used for direct patient care, so use a clinically embedded version. Do not use a risk tool for type 1 diabetes, eGFR below 60 and/or albuminuria, or inherited lipid disorders.

    2

    Check before a statin

    ASSESS

    +

    Discuss absolute benefit, harms, preferences, frailty, comorbidity and interactions. Check full lipids, diabetes status, renal function, transaminases and clinical risks; TSH if thyroid symptoms. Ask about persistent generalised unexplained muscle symptoms before offering a statin.

    3

    Primary prevention branch

    DRUG

    +

    Support lifestyle changes and other modifiable risks. Offer atorvastatin 20 mg if QRISK3 is at least 10%; a lower score does not rule treatment out when risk is underestimated or an informed person prefers it. Type 1 diabetes (no risk tool): offer statin treatment to adults over 40, with diabetes for more than 10 years, established nephropathy or other CVD risk factors; consider for ages 18–40; when starting, use atorvastatin 20 mg. Do not routinely offer aspirin. BNF adult primary-prevention dose: 20 mg once daily; if needed increase at intervals of at least 4 weeks, maximum 80 mg once daily; prevention doses differ from product licences. Check interactions and muscle/liver risk; avoid in pregnancy, breastfeeding and active liver disease. MHRA (September 2023) notes very infrequent reports of new or worsening myasthenia gravis with statins. CKD starting branch is 20 mg; higher dose needs renal-specialist agreement if eGFR is below 30.

    4

    Secondary prevention / CKD branch

    DRUG

    +

    Established CVD: offer atorvastatin 80 mg, or a lower dose for interactions, high adverse-effect risk or preference (off-label in December 2023 per NICE). CKD has a separate starting dose of 20 mg; agree higher doses with a renal specialist if eGFR is below 30. BNF adult secondary-prevention dose: 80 mg once daily; prevention use is outside the product licence. The same interaction, muscle/liver, pregnancy/breastfeeding and myasthenia-gravis cautions as the primary branch apply.

    5

    Targets and additional treatment

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Primary prevention: aim for a greater than 40% non-HDL cholesterol reduction. Secondary: LDL at most 2.0 mmol/L or non-HDL at most 2.6 mmol/L. If the target is not met, discuss adherence and dose timing, encourage lifestyle improvement and consider increasing statin intensity/dose before escalation; use the full NICE technology appraisal criteria for non-statin treatment (ezetimibe, bempedoic acid combinations, alirocumab, evolocumab, inclisiran).

    6

    Review response and safety

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Check lipids and transaminases 2–3 months after starting or changing treatment; transaminases at 12 months, then only if clinically indicated. Provide an annual medication review. Advise people to seek medical advice for unexplained muscle symptoms and measure creatine kinase if they occur; do not measure CK in asymptomatic people. Do not stop statins because of a raised blood glucose or HbA1c.

    Alternative (non-UK) guidance – for comparison only

    Not UK guidance. Shown because newer non-UK guidance or trials report better outcomes for this problem. Do not use it in place of the UK chart unless a local specialist pathway adopts it. AI-assisted review, not clinical sign-off.

    What differs: ESC/EAS (2019, focused update 2025) sets LDL-C goals by risk category. For very-high-risk people (secondary prevention or, rarely, primary prevention), the goal is below 1.4 mmol/L and at least a 50% reduction from baseline. NICE NG238 aims for LDL-C of 2.0 mmol/L or less (or non-HDL-C of 2.6 mmol/L or less) in secondary prevention.

    Evidence for better outcomes: FOURIER (NEJM 2017; 27,564 people with ASCVD on statins): evolocumab lowered median LDL-C to 0.78 mmol/L. CV death, MI, stroke, unstable-angina admission or revascularisation: HR 0.85 (0.79–0.92). CV death, MI or stroke: HR 0.80 (0.73–0.88). Adverse events did not differ significantly from placebo.

    UK licensing and availability: Ezetimibe, PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies and inclisiran are UK-licensed. NHS access to PCSK9-targeted treatment is restricted by NICE technology appraisal criteria, so a lower target does not by itself make someone eligible. NICE TA694 (updated 24 June 2026) recommends bempedoic acid with ezetimibe only if statins are contraindicated or not tolerated and ezetimibe alone does not control LDL-C well enough. NICE NG238 1.1.7 uses QRISK3, not SCORE2, to estimate 10-year risk.

    1

    Classify risk (ESC/EAS)

    ASSESS

    +

    Very high, high, moderate or low risk (ESC/EAS 2025 Table 3, which replaces 2019 Table 4). Very high risk includes: documented ASCVD, clinical or unequivocal on imaging (significant plaque on angiography, CT or carotid or femoral ultrasound, or a markedly elevated CAC score, for example above 300); diabetes with target organ damage, or at least three major risk factors, or early-onset type 1 diabetes of long duration (over 20 years); severe CKD (eGFR below 30); SCORE2 or SCORE2-OP of 20% or more for 10-year fatal or non-fatal CVD; familial hypercholesterolaemia with ASCVD or another major risk factor. High risk includes: markedly elevated single risk factors (total cholesterol above 8 mmol/L, LDL-C above 4.9 mmol/L, or BP 180/110 or more); familial hypercholesterolaemia without other major risk factors; diabetes without target organ damage but lasting 10 years or more or with another risk factor; moderate CKD (eGFR 30 to 59); SCORE2 or SCORE2-OP of 10% to below 20%. Moderate risk: SCORE2 or SCORE2-OP of 2% to below 10%, or a young person with diabetes (type 1 under 35, type 2 under 50) of under 10 years duration and no other risk factors. Low risk: SCORE2 or SCORE2-OP below 2%. The SCORE2 bands are not for people with existing ASCVD or already on lipid-lowering treatment.

    2

    Set the LDL-C goal

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Very high risk (secondary or primary prevention): at least a 50% LDL-C reduction from baseline and an LDL-C goal below 1.4 mmol/L. High risk: at least a 50% reduction and a goal below 1.8 mmol/L. Goals checked against the 2019 guideline full text; the 2025 focused update full text and the ESC Cardiopractice summary both state that the LDL-C treatment goals have not changed from 2019.

    3

    Escalate stepwise

    DRUG

    +

    Statin at the highest tolerated dose. If the goal is not reached, add non-statin therapy with proven benefit (ezetimibe, a PCSK9 monoclonal antibody or bempedoic acid, alone or in combination); the 2025 update says to choose by how much more LDL-C lowering is needed, patient preference, availability and cost. After an acute coronary syndrome: intensify any existing lipid-lowering treatment during the admission (Class I, level C), and consider starting high-intensity statin plus ezetimibe together in people not already treated who are not expected to reach goal on a statin alone (Class IIa, level B). In the UK, PCSK9-targeted treatment only within NICE technology appraisal criteria.

    1

    Assess needs and risk

    ASSESS

    +

    Adults 18+ with suspected or established GAD; not a panic-disorder, perinatal mental-health or crisis-response protocol. Consider GAD-2, then a full assessment of distress, functioning, comorbidity, substance use, physical illness and previous treatment. Consider step-4 referral for severe anxiety with marked impairment plus self-harm/suicide risk, self-neglect, significant comorbidity or inadequate step-3 response.

    2

    Education and active monitoring

    SUPPORT

    +

    Explain GAD and treatment options and monitor symptoms and functioning. Treat the more severe primary disorder when depression or another anxiety disorder coexists; harmful or dependent substance misuse is treated first.

    3

    Persistent symptoms

    SUPPORT

    +

    low intensity: After education and active monitoring without improvement, offer individual non-facilitated self-help, individual guided self-help or psychoeducational groups (all CBT-based), guided by preference. This is not an automatic medicine-first pathway.

    4

    Marked impairment or non-response

    DRUG

    +

    Offer high-intensity CBT/applied relaxation OR drug treatment, by preference after a benefits/risks discussion. If a drug, offer an SSRI; consider sertraline first (most cost-effective) noting off-label GAD use with documented informed consent. Sertraline (CKS GAD regimen, April 2025): 25 mg once daily for 1 week, then 50 mg daily; if needed increase by 50 mg at intervals of at least 1 week to maximum 200 mg daily; check MAOI/serotonergic interactions, bleeding and hyponatraemia risk, suicidality, hepatic impairment, withdrawal. Escitalopram (licensed, BNF; Sussex Formulary 4.3.4 lists it as 'Third choice for generalised anxiety disorder', accessed 21 November 2026): 10 mg once daily, max 20 mg; older adults initially 5 mg, max 10 mg; mild/moderate hepatic impairment initially 5 mg for 2 weeks then 10 mg by response, extra caution in severe; CYP2C19 poor metabolisers initially 5 mg for 2 weeks then up to 10 mg (UK SmPC). Check QT/congenital long-QT contraindications, other QT drugs, serotonergic medicines, sodium/bleeding, early suicidality, renal/hepatic factors. Explain initial anxiety increase, delayed benefit over 1 week or more, and withdrawal.

    5

    Medicine cautions and alternatives

    DRUG

    +

    Discuss activation, withdrawal, interactions and bleeding risk (gastroprotection if older or on NSAIDs/aspirin). If sertraline is ineffective, offer an alternative SSRI or SNRI. Duloxetine (licensed, BNF): initially 30 mg once daily, usual maintenance 60 mg, max 120 mg daily; avoid in hepatic impairment, CrCl under 30 mL/min and uncontrolled hypertension; taper, do not stop abruptly. Venlafaxine MR (licensed, BNF): 75 mg once daily, increase at intervals of at least 2 weeks, max 225 mg daily; control BP first, contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension; withdrawal can be marked. If SSRIs/SNRIs not tolerated, consider pregabalin (Class C Schedule 3): 150 mg daily in 2–3 divided doses, increase in steps of 150 mg at weekly intervals to max 600 mg daily; assess abuse/dependence history, agree a treatment-ending strategy (MHRA January 2026), renal dose reduction, respiratory depression risk with opioids/other depressants; follow MHRA pregnancy advice. Benzodiazepines only short term during crises; do not offer an antipsychotic for GAD in primary care.

    6

    Early and ongoing follow-up

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    All ages (MHRA Drug Safety Update, 15 October 2026): when starting or changing the dose, or at the next review, discuss the risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviours and that some antidepressants can cause sexual dysfunction that may persist after stopping ('PSSD'); advise urgent medical attention for new or worsening suicidal intent. Under 30 starting an SSRI/SNRI: warn about increased suicidal-thinking/self-harm risk, see within 1 week of first prescribing, monitor weekly for the first month. Review effectiveness and side effects every 2–4 weeks for the first 3 months, then every 3 months. If effective, continue for at least 1 year. Immediate danger or self-harm needs emergency/crisis care.

    1

    Evaluate secondary causes first

    URGENT

    +

    Selected warning features, not a complete screen: sudden maximum within 5 minutes, fever/worsening, new neurological/cognitive/personality change, altered consciousness, trauma, cough/Valsalva/sneeze, exertional/postural trigger, GCA/glaucoma features or major pattern change. New-onset headache has additional malignancy/immunity/vomiting criteria: open both source lists before deciding investigation/referral.

    2

    Make a positive diagnosis

    ASSESS

    +

    Use the original feature table for migraine/tension/cluster classification; atypical aura needs investigation/referral consideration. Consider a diary for at least 8 weeks recording pattern, associated symptoms, medicines and triggers. Imaging is not for reassurance alone after a primary-headache diagnosis.

    3

    Acute migraine: choose together

    DRUG

    +

    Offer oral triptan + NSAID, or triptan + paracetamol, accounting for preference and risks. If one drug preferred, consider triptan/NSAID/aspirin/paracetamol alone. Consider an antiemetic even without nausea. No opioids or ergots.

    4

    Preventive treatment if appropriate

    DRUG

    +

    Consider propranolol, topiramate or amitriptyline after full suitability discussion; no automatic first choice. Consider propranolol overdose risk with depression. Topiramate is not for migraine prevention in pregnancy; anyone who could become pregnant requires the Pregnancy Prevention Programme: highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least 4 weeks after stopping; topiramate can reduce the efficacy of systemic hormonal contraceptives – also use a barrier method and report bleeding-pattern changes (SmPC 4.5); give the Patient Guide; repeat pregnancy testing during treatment as needed; the PPP applies even to women not currently sexually active unless the prescriber records compelling reasons for no pregnancy risk; if pregnancy occurs on topiramate for migraine – stop straight away, contact the GP urgently, and arrange prompt specialist reassessment (MHRA/DSU; SmPC 4.6); if planning pregnancy – see the GP or specialist before stopping topiramate or contraception. Breastfeeding: decide between suspending breastfeeding and discontinuing or abstaining from topiramate, weighing the benefit of each; observed infant effects include diarrhoea, drowsiness, irritability and inadequate weight gain (SmPC 4.6). Dose framing (side by side, not decided): BNF usual 50–100 mg/day, maximum 200 mg/day; SmPC recommended total daily dose 100 mg/day, benefit seen at 50 mg/day, up to 200 mg/day received with caution about side effects. (Topamax SmPC emc 1977 wording; the 25 mg nightly 1-week start, 25 mg weekly increments and CrCl <=70 mL/min half-dose rule agree with the SmPC. Follow the dispensed product's SmPC and record which framing guided titration.)

    5

    Prevent medication overuse

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Suspect overuse when headache develops/worsens with at least 3 months of triptan/opioid/ergot/combination use on 10+ days/month, or simple analgesic/NSAID use on 15+ days/month. Explain risk and use the full supported withdrawal/referral pathway.

    6

    Review; escalate selected cases

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Review prevention at 3–6 months. CGRP inhibitors: stop at 12 weeks for <50% episodic or <30% chronic migraine-frequency reduction. Botulinum toxin: follow the distinct 2-cycle/episodic-conversion stopping rules. Use diary, tolerability and the full appraisal.

    7

    Acute and preventive appraisal routes

    DRUG

    +

    Acute: TA1172 atogepant or TA919 rimegepant after ≥2 triptans failed, or triptans unsuitable plus NSAID/paracetamol failure. Prevention: CGRP options have ≥3 preventive failures/unsuitability and frequency criteria; chronic-migraine botulinum toxin also requires medication-overuse management.

    Decide place of care first

    URGENT

    +

    Use NG115 Table 7 (all rows checked against the full-guideline PDF). Factors favouring hospital care: severe breathlessness; general condition poor/deteriorating; level of activity poor/confined to bed; cyanosis; worsening peripheral oedema; impaired level of consciousness; acute confusion; rapid rate of onset; already receiving long-term oxygen therapy; social circumstances living alone/not coping; unable to cope at home; significant comorbidity (particularly cardiac disease and insulin-dependent diabetes); SaO2 below 90%; changes on chest radiograph; arterial pH below 7.35; arterial PaO2 below 7 kPa. Home-care factors are the converse for each row.

    2

    Assess the exacerbation

    ASSESS

    +

    Compare with usual symptoms; assess possible pneumonia and other serious causes. Pulse oximetry is useful with severe features but may overestimate borderline readings in darker skin; it does not show CO2 or pH.

    3

    Inhaled treatment, oxygen rules and oral steroids

    DRUG

    +

    Inhaler/nebuliser choice depends on dose, device ability and supervision (NG115 1.3.8/1.3.9). NEBULISER GAS (1.3.11/1.3.12): if the person is hypercapnic or acidotic, the nebuliser should be driven by COMPRESSED AIR rather than oxygen (to avoid worsening hypercapnia) – if oxygen therapy is needed, administer it simultaneously by nasal cannulae; the driving gas for nebulised therapy should ALWAYS be specified in the prescription. OXYGEN TARGET (1.3.27): if necessary, prescribe oxygen to keep the SaO2 within the INDIVIDUALISED TARGET RANGE. Pulse oximetry gives NO information about PaCO2 or pH (1.3.28). Table 7 thresholds that favour hospital care include: SaO2 below 90%, arterial pH below 7.35, arterial PaO2 below 7 kPa (Table 7). ORAL STEROIDS: if there are no contraindications, consider oral corticosteroids for people in the community with a significant increase in breathlessness that interferes with daily activities – offer 30 mg oral prednisolone once daily for 5 days (NG115 1.3.14 and 1.3.16; CKS COPD acute-exacerbation scenario, last revised December 2025). Discuss adverse effects of prolonged therapy, and consider the need for osteoporosis prophylaxis in people requiring frequent courses of oral corticosteroids (3–4 courses per year) (same CKS page).

    4

    Antibiotics: the full NG114 Table 1 oral ladder

    DRUG

    +

    Antibiotics are not automatic: a range of factors (including viral infections and smoking) can trigger an exacerbation, and many exacerbations (including some severe exacerbations) are not caused by bacterial infections so will not respond to antibiotics (NG114 1.1.1). Consider an antibiotic only after taking into account: the severity of symptoms, particularly sputum colour changes and increases in volume or thickness beyond the person's normal day-to-day variation; whether they may need to go into hospital for treatment; previous exacerbation and hospital admission history, and the risk of developing complications; previous sputum culture and susceptibility results; and the risk of antimicrobial resistance with repeated courses of antibiotics (NG114 1.1.2). Oral first line if suitable (NG114 Table 1). FIRST-CHOICE oral (empirical or guided by most recent sputum culture): amoxicillin 500 mg three times a day for 5 days; doxycycline 200 mg on first day then 100 mg once a day (5-day course in total); clarithromycin 500 mg twice a day for 5 days. SECOND-CHOICE (no improvement on first choice taken for at least 2–3 days): use an alternative first choice FROM A DIFFERENT CLASS. ALTERNATIVE choices if at higher risk of treatment failure (repeated antibiotic courses, resistant bacteria on sputum culture, higher complication risk): co-amoxiclav 500/125 mg three times a day for 5 days; co-trimoxazole 960 mg twice a day for 5 days (only with bacteriological evidence of sensitivity and good reason to prefer it – BNF caveat); levofloxacin 500 mg once a day for 5 days ONLY if other alternatives unsuitable, with specialist advice (MHRA January 2024 fluoroquinolone restrictions). If the person is on antibiotic prophylaxis, treat with an antibiotic from a different class. IV antibiotics: review by 48 hours and step down to oral where possible.

    Response and cultures

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    If antibiotic given, seek help for worsening, systemic illness or failure to start improving in 2–3 days. Reassess and send sputum if not improved after antibiotic treatment and not already sampled. Change for resistant results only if not already improving.

    6

    Escalate repeated failure: specialist triggers and recovery review

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Seek specialist advice for an acute exacerbation of COPD if symptoms are NOT IMPROVING WITH REPEATED COURSES of antibiotics, or bacteria are RESISTANT TO ORAL antibiotics, or the person CANNOT TAKE ORAL medicines (NG114 1.1.8). If no antibiotic is given, advise: an antibiotic is not needed currently, and to seek medical help WITHOUT DELAY if symptoms worsen rapidly or significantly, or the person becomes systemically unwell (1.1.5, close paraphrase). RECOVERY: give people (particularly those discharged from hospital) clear instructions on why, when and how to stop their corticosteroid treatment (NG115 1.3.20); monitor recovery by regular clinical assessment of symptoms and observation of functional capacity (1.3.37).

    URGENTREFER

    +

    Refer to hospital for a suspected more serious condition such as cellulitis, or widespread impetigo with immunocompromise. Consider referral/advice for bullous impetigo (particularly babies aged ≤1 year), frequent recurrence, systemic illness or high complication risk. The treatment branches below are adult-focused.

    2

    Localised non-bullous: the named topical options

    DRUG

    +

    If not systemically unwell/high risk, consider hydrogen peroxide 1% cream – apply two or three times a day for 5 days (NG153 Table 1). If hydrogen peroxide is unsuitable (for example, impetigo around the eyes) or ineffective: fusidic acid 2% cream – apply three times a day for 5 days (first-choice topical antibiotic). If fusidic acid resistance is suspected or confirmed: mupirocin 2% – apply three times a day for 5 days. Advise people with impetigo, and their parents or carers if appropriate, about good hygiene measures to reduce the spread of impetigo to other areas of the body and to other people (NG153 1.1.1).

    3

    Widespread or oral branch: named oral options

    DRUG

    +

    Widespread non-bullous: offer a short topical OR oral antibiotic course. ORAL (NG153 Table 1, close paraphrase): first choice flucloxacillin 500 mg four times a day for 5 days; penicillin allergy or flucloxacillin unsuitable (not pregnant) – clarithromycin 250 mg twice a day for 5 days (can be increased to 500 mg twice a day for severe infections); penicillin allergy in pregnancy – erythromycin 250 mg to 500 mg four times a day for 5 days. Administration (flucloxacillin SmPC section 4.2): take on an EMPTY STOMACH – at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals; swallow capsules with a FULL GLASS OF WATER (250 ml) to reduce the risk of oesophageal pain; patients should NOT LIE DOWN immediately after intake. Hypokalaemia monitoring (SmPC section 4.4): potentially life-threatening hypokalaemia can occur, especially in high doses, and can be resistant to potassium supplementation; regular measurements of potassium levels are recommended during therapy with higher doses – take care with hypokalaemia-inducing diuretics or other risk factors (e.g. malnutrition, renal tubule dysfunction).

    4

    Bullous or unwell/high risk

    DRUG

    +

    Offer a short oral antibiotic course for all bullous impetigo, or non-bullous disease with systemic illness/high risk. Choose from the original adult table, guided by allergy and local resistance; flucloxacillin is first-choice oral treatment.

    5

    If treatment fails

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Reassess worsening or no improvement after the course. After topical-antibiotic failure, offer an oral antibiotic and consider a swab; after oral failure consider swab. Revisit herpes simplex, cellulitis and resistance rather than repeating blindly.

    6

    Frequent recurrence / culture review

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Send skin swab for frequent recurrence; consider nasal swab/decolonisation with the full pathway. Review culture results and use a narrow-spectrum alternative if symptoms are not improving.
