The polysymptomatic patient: assess, prioritise, review

Adults presenting with several physical symptoms, whether new, recurrent or persistent. Multiple symptoms are a presentation, not a diagnosis of medically unexplained symptoms. Physical disease, medicine effects, functional symptoms and mental-health problems may coexist. This is not a paediatric pathway.

1

Check what cannot wait

Assess clinical stability and symptom-specific warning features before negotiating a routine agenda. Act on acute deterioration or an urgent physical, mental-health or safeguarding concern. Use the relevant emergency/urgent pathway; do not delay it to finish this chart.

+

Scope: This is not an exhaustive red-flag checklist. Urgency depends on the actual presentation, observations and clinical judgement.

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: targeted assessment, red flags and risk prioritisation
  • NG12: symptom-specific cancer investigation and referral
2

Hear the whole agenda, then agree priorities

Let the person describe their symptoms and ask what else they wanted to discuss. Clarify their main worry, desired help and effect on life. Summarise the full list, explain what can safely be covered today, and agree follow-up for remaining concerns rather than dismissing them.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: symptom history, concerns, expectations and impact
  • RCGP: targeted data gathering and patient-centred prioritisation
3

Build one timeline, not disconnected complaints

Map onset, course, triggers, associated symptoms and changes from baseline. Review prior records, examinations, tests and referrals before duplicating them. Ask what previous results meant to the person. Reassess new findings even if earlier tests were normal.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: assessment and symptom history
  • NG193 1.1: re-evaluate changed chronic pain
  • RCGP: use existing information and revise hypotheses
4

Explore function and context without assuming cause

Ask about sleep, daily activity, work/caring roles, mood, anxiety, stressors, support and coping. Explore sensitive issues with permission. Physical and psychological problems can interact; neither distress nor normal tests proves that a symptom is psychological.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: symptoms are real; physical and psychological factors
  • NG56 1.5–1.6: disease burden, goals and context
  • NG193 1.1: person-centred assessment
5

Check medicines and treatment burden

Reconcile prescribed, over-the-counter and other substances, recent changes, adverse effects, adherence and allergies. Where multimorbidity applies, review benefits, harms and the burden of multiple appointments. Agree changes safely; do not stop medicines solely because several symptoms are present.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: medicines can cause symptoms
  • NG56 1.6: treatment burden and medicines review
6

Examine and test to answer a clinical question

Choose observations, examination and investigations from the history, differential and relevant condition guidance. Explain what each test can and cannot show and who will act on results. There is no single blood panel that diagnoses or excludes every cause of multiple symptoms.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: physical assessment and possible tests
  • RCGP: targeted information and diagnostic reasoning
  • GMC 69–71: record findings, decisions and actions
7

Keep the differential open

Consider a unifying condition, several coexisting conditions and medicine effects. Use the relevant diagnostic pathway when a recognisable pattern emerges. A normal investigation is not itself a diagnosis of functional symptoms, fibromyalgia, ME/CFS or chronic primary pain.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: possible explanations and named syndromes
  • NG193 1.1.4–1.1.7: clinical judgement and coexistence
  • NG206 1.2: symptom pattern and assessment
8

Do not miss non-specific cancer features

Apply current NG12 to the actual symptoms, age and findings. Its 2026 recommendation for age 60+ with unexplained weight loss greater than 5% over 6 months calls for further assessment and urgent investigation or an appropriate cancer/non-specific-symptoms referral. Unexplained appetite loss has a separate recommendation. Younger age is not reassurance against other concerning features.

+

Scope: Use the full source. This box does not replace other cancer criteria or safety-netting.

Sources and qualifications

  • NG12 1.13.2–1.13.4: exact scope and routes
9

Explain findings and uncertainty honestly

Acknowledge that symptoms are real and disruptive. Explain the working assessment, what has and has not been ruled out, and what happens next. When chronic pain is present, avoid making normal results sound as though the experience is invalid. Do not promise that further investigation will never be needed.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: explaining persistent symptoms
  • NG193 1.1.13–1.1.19: information, validation and shared plan
10

Agree a function-focused, tailored plan

Treat identified conditions and agree achievable goals, support and review. Offer relevant psychological care by indication and preference, not as proof symptoms are imagined. Avoid blanket exercise advice: if post-exertional malaise suggests ME/CFS, follow NG206 and do not prescribe fixed incremental exercise increases.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NHS: shared goals and psychological support
  • NG56: individualised management plan
  • NG206 1.2 and 1.11: recognition and activity safeguards
11

Refer for a reason, not a symptom count

Choose a service to answer a clinical question: urgent disease assessment, diagnostic uncertainty or a condition-specific specialist need. Do not automatically send every multisymptom patient to rheumatology or mental health. Explain the purpose, urgency, interim plan and what to do while waiting; use advice-and-guidance only if appropriate, never instead of indicated urgent care.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • GMC: relevant history, referral purpose and responsibility
  • RCGP: appropriate referral and coordinated care
12

Close the loop and book continuity

Agree who reviews unresolved symptoms, test results, treatment effects and referrals, and when. Give specific warning signs and an access route for worsening or non-response. Record what was deferred, the reason and next appointment. Reassess changed symptoms rather than labelling every new problem as the old condition.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • NG56 1.6.17: plan, coordination and follow-up
  • NG193 1.1.22–1.1.23: reassess a change or flare
  • GMC: patient information and responsibility

Patient information to share selectively

Choose information after assessment. Do not give a medically-unexplained-symptoms leaflet solely because a person has several symptoms. The NICE medicines page is for multimorbidity, not a universal symptom pathway.

Sources, dates and limits

An authored consultation aid, not a validated diagnostic pathway. NG56 applies to people with two or more long-term conditions, not simply two symptoms. NG193 applies to chronic pain and NG206 to suspected/confirmed ME/CFS, not all persistent symptoms. No universal blood panel, symptom-count diagnosis, psychiatric attribution, medicine choice or fixed referral waiting time is supplied. No local clinical difference was verified for this general approach, so no National / Local tabs are added. Check current local services when making an individual referral.

3 domainsDG&D · CM&C · Relating to others

Data gathering and diagnosis; Clinical management and medical complexity; Relating to others. These apply across the conversation, not in isolated time blocks.

Admin is different: exam notes are private; real practice needs a checked clinical record and completed referral workflow. The 2–3 / 3–5 minute admin allowances below are suggested planning estimates, not official exam times.

1

Official format: 3 + 12 minutes

Each of 12 simulated consultations has 3 minutes of reading time followed by a 12-minute video or telephone consultation. The platform gives a one-minute warning. The 3 minutes prepare for the next case; do not count them as a post-case notes or referral-writing allowance.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP examination-day schedule and platform guide
2

Before the call: use the reading time

Read every page of the brief, identify the task, relevant history, medicines and results, then form initial hypotheses and questions. Private typed notes carry into the consultation and are not visible to examiners. This is preparation, not a substitute for checking the patient's agenda.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: brief, private notes and reading time
  • RCGP: use existing medical information
3

0–1 minute: connect and set the agenda

Confirm who you are speaking to and the context as appropriate. Start openly, hear the concern, notice cues and check for additional issues. Agree the focus without shutting down an urgent or important concern.

+

Scope: Suggested coaching checkpoint, not an official RCGP time target.

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: relating to others and targeted data gathering
4

1–3 minutes: story, impact and concerns

Clarify symptoms or the issue presented, patient ideas, concerns and expectations. Ask about impact and the context that will change decisions. Use active listening and purposeful questions rather than running a generic checklist.

+

Scope: Suggested coaching checkpoint; communication continues throughout.

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: DG&D and relating to others
5

3–6 minutes: targeted assessment

Test your hypotheses with focused history, relevant red flags, background, medicines and supplied examination or test information. For remote cases, explain if an in-person examination is needed; never invent findings. Adjust the pace for complexity and safety.

+

Scope: Suggested coaching checkpoint, not a hard stop on needed assessment.

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: diagnostic reasoning and patient safety
  • RCGP: remote consultation format
6

Around 6–7 minutes: summarise and explain

Check your understanding, share the working diagnosis or analysis and acknowledge uncertainty. Aim to leave enough time for a real discussion of management. The RCGP-linked overview suggests finishing data gathering around 6–7 minutes; this is educational guidance, not a standalone marking rule.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP toolkit overview: original educational guidance
  • RCGP: diagnosis and shared understanding
7

7–10½ minutes: agree a workable plan

Discuss appropriate options, benefits/harms and the person's preference. Include investigations, treatment, self-care or a referral when needed. For a referral, explain why, urgency, what the service may do and the interim plan. Check feasibility, comorbidity and concerns.

+

Scope: Suggested coaching checkpoint. Do not promise a service, shared care or waiting time you have not checked.

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: clinical management and complexity
  • RCGP toolkit: patient-centred management
  • GMC: explaining referral
8

10½–12 minutes: safety-net and follow up

Agree specific warning signs, where and when to seek help, who handles results/referrals, and planned review. Check understanding and unresolved questions. This is part of clinical management, not a rushed phrase at the buzzer.

+

Scope: Suggested coaching checkpoint, not a mandatory 90-second closing script.

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: safety, continuity and follow-up
  • GMC: responsibilities and patient information
9

Exam notes and referrals: say the plan

Use private notes sparingly without losing the conversation. The official schedule does not list a separate post-case clinical-note or referral-letter station. Communicate the proposed referral, rationale, urgency and follow-up within the consultation. Do not confuse unmarked private notes with a real clinical record.

+

Sources and qualifications

  • RCGP: schedule and notes-window visibility
  • RCGP: assessment of management and continuity
10

Real notes: try a 2–3 minute allowance

For a straightforward encounter, start by reserving about 2–3 minutes to check and complete a concise record, then measure and adjust with your trainer. This is an author-suggested planning estimate, not a national standard. Complex, safeguarding, interpreter or uncertainty-heavy consultations may need much longer. Record during or as soon as possible after care.

+

Scope: Include relevant findings, assessment/uncertainty, preferences, information given, decisions/actions, review and author/time. Record only what actually happened.

Sources and qualifications

  • GMC 69–71 and footnote 6: proportionate contemporaneous records
11

Real referrals: reserve separate time

A simple routine referral with records ready might need a 3–5 minute planning allowance; complex letters, forms and coordination can take longer. This estimate is not RCGP/GMC guidance. Emergency or time-critical handover happens immediately, not in a later admin block. Do not mark a referral sent when it is only planned.

+

Scope: Check the receiving service, question, urgency, relevant history/examination/tests, medicines/allergies, patient understanding and information-sharing needs. Track submission and response.

Sources and qualifications

  • GMC: safe referral, relevant information and accountability
  • GMC: record agreed actions accurately
12

A 20-minute real-clinic planning example

An illustrative block is 12 minutes for the patient, 3 for notes and 5 for a straightforward referral. If no referral is needed, use the remaining time for other clinical work or review. This is arithmetic and a training suggestion, not an appointment-length recommendation. A multi-symptom consultation may need a longer booked review or double appointment.

+

Scope: Do not compress necessary assessment to fit this example. Agree workload and protected admin arrangements locally; urgent care takes priority.

Sources and qualifications

  • GMC: safe, accurate records
  • NG56: coordinated, individualised care where applicable
