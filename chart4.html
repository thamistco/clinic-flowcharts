National / Local guidance

What differs: Documented source conflict: the national qualifying-contract rule is wider than the local FAQ wording.

Safety and source status: Use the national legal criteria, not a narrower local provider-list restriction. Do not cancel an existing referral without an agreed handover.

National guidance

NHS England: for a clinically appropriate referral where the legal right applies, a provider can hold a qualifying NHS Standard Contract with any ICB or NHS England for the required service. Full eligibility and exceptions apply; this is not a blanket entitlement to any private provider.

  • NHS England patient-choice guidance

Local guidance and access

The Surrey and Sussex FAQ describes contract scope more narrowly. That does not override NHS England guidance. Local referral/age tables and shared-care arrangements still matter operationally: confirm the exact service, follow-up and prescribing responsibility; GP shared care and shorter waits are not guaranteed.

1

Hot swollen joint: exclude infection

A sudden severely painful, hot/swollen joint needs urgent assessment; fever may be absent. If septic arthritis is suspected, refer immediately via the local pathway. Do not label a first painful red joint as gout without considering infection and CPP crystal arthritis.

Other urgent features

Major injury with severe pain, deformity, inability to bear weight or altered sensation needs emergency assessment. New headache/scalp tenderness, jaw/tongue claudication or visual symptoms with a PMR-type presentation suggest GCA and need immediate medical attention.

3

Describe the pattern, not a label

Record onset/duration, joints and symmetry, swelling/heat, morning/rest stiffness, activity effects, function and systemic symptoms. Ask about skin/nails, eye symptoms, bowel/genitourinary illness and family history when SpA is possible. Look for true synovitis, enthesitis and dactylitis.

Persistent synovitis: RA route

Refer any adult with unexplained persistent synovitis. Urgent referral if small hand/foot joints, more than one joint, or ≥3 months delay before seeking advice, even with normal inflammatory markers or negative RF/anti-CCP. Do not wait for tests to refer.

5

Investigations should fit the question

With clinical synovitis/suspected RA: RF; consider anti-CCP if RF negative; hand/foot X-rays for persistent synovitis. These must not delay referral. For suspected gout, measure serum urate; consider aspiration if uncertain, and repeat low flare urate ≥2 weeks after settling. No blanket diagnostic blood panel.

OA: activity-related pattern

NG226 clinical OA diagnosis: age ≥45, activity-related joint pain and no morning stiffness or stiffness ≤30 minutes. Do not routinely image unless atypical features or another diagnosis is suspected. A hot swollen joint or prolonged stiffness should not be forced into an OA label.

7

Gout or CPP crystal arthritis

Rapid severe red/swollen episodes, often overnight, support crystal disease but are not proof. Gout often involves first MTP; CPP attacks commonly involve knee/wrist. A serum urate alone does not distinguish all causes. Use aspiration/imaging when needed and keep infection on the differential.

8

Psoriatic / peripheral SpA

Ask about psoriasis/nails, dactylitis, enthesitis, uveitis and IBD. Urgently refer suspected new inflammatory arthritis via its appropriate route. Refer dactylitis; refer non-mechanical enthesitis if persistent, multisite OR linked to NG65 associated features. Open the full feature list. Painful red light-sensitive/blurred eye needs same-day ophthalmology.

Inflammatory back pain: exact SpA criteria

For back pain starting before 45 and lasting >3 months, use the full NG65 additional-feature list: refer if ≥4; if exactly 3, test HLA-B27 and refer if positive. Do not improvise a shortened score. Negative tests or normal X-rays do not alone exclude SpA.

10

PMR: shoulder/hip girdle pattern

Consider in over-50s with bilateral shoulder/pelvic girdle pain and prolonged stiffness; check mimics, inflammatory markers and GCA. For confirmed PMR, 2026 BSR suggests discussing first relapse with rheumatology and referring after two relapses or ≥18 months of glucocorticoids, alongside other indications. No diagnostic steroid trial is supplied here.

11

Reactive or post-viral patterns

Recent gastrointestinal/genitourinary infection can precede reactive arthritis; post-viral arthritis can mimic RA. Use history, examination and targeted infection testing where indicated, not an assumed benign diagnosis. Persistent synovitis still needs appropriate referral; eye symptoms may be urgent.

Widespread pain / fibromyalgia

Widespread pain, fatigue and unrefreshing sleep can suggest fibromyalgia; there is no single diagnostic test. Examine for alternative disease, especially synovitis, and assess symptoms/function. Fibromyalgia and inflammatory arthritis can coexist; do not dismiss new swelling because of an existing diagnosis.

Fibromyalgia: diagnosis, support and review

Adults with suspected or established fibromyalgia. RCP supports diagnosis; NICE NG193 supports management of chronic primary pain in over-16s, with separate medicines advice for ages 16–17 and 18+. This adult page is not a paediatric pathway.

1

Listen and map the impact

Acknowledge pain as real. Ask about widespread pain, fatigue, unrefreshing sleep, cognitive symptoms, daily function, mood, work, caring roles, strengths and goals. Allow enough time; a further appointment may be needed. Normal tests do not invalidate pain.

2

Look for other disease and urgent concerns

Do not attribute new focal neurological findings, systemic illness, unexplained weight loss, hot swollen joints or other concerning changes to fibromyalgia. Assess the symptom-specific red flags and arrange urgent/immediate assessment when indicated. Fibromyalgia can coexist with other disease.

3

Clinical diagnosis, not a blood-test label

An adequately experienced clinician can make the diagnosis; specialist confirmation is not compulsory in a clear case. There is no confirmatory test and it is not simply a diagnosis of exclusion. Use history/examination, ideally face to face, and consider mimics and coexisting conditions.

4

Use the full RCP diagnostic worksheet

RCP uses ACR 2016 criteria: WPI ≥7 with SSS ≥5, or WPI 4–6 with SSS ≥9; pain in at least 4 of 5 regions; symptoms at a similar level for at least 3 months. Use the full definitions and clinician judgement. No self-completed score alone or online auto-diagnosis.

5

Investigate mimics without endless retesting

RCP lists FBC, ESR/CRP, CK, LFTs, TSH, blood glucose and U&Es to assess alternatives/coexisting disease. Further tests depend on clinical suspicion. Explain the purpose; avoid repeating investigations after diagnosis without new symptoms/signs. A normal panel does not itself diagnose fibromyalgia.

Explain and agree a shared plan

Explain the working diagnosis, uncertainty, likely fluctuation and what management can improve. Agree meaningful function/quality-of-life goals, written information and support. Treat coexisting disease in parallel. Do not imply symptoms are imaginary or promise a cure.

Tailored activity and psychological care

Offer a supervised group exercise programme for chronic primary pain, adapted to needs, preferences and abilities; support ongoing activity. Consider ACT or CBT for pain from appropriately trained staff. Review tolerance and goals rather than imposing a fixed exercise escalation.

Other non-drug options: preserve limits

NG193 allows consideration of one course of acupuncture/dry needling only within its stated training, setting and cost/time limits; use the original recommendation. Do not offer TENS, ultrasound, interferential therapy or biofeedback for chronic primary pain.

Medicines: chronic primary pain is distinct

For age ≥18, NICE says consider one of its specified antidepressants after discussing benefits/harms, even without depression. NG193/CKS describe off-label use; check the current product licence, BNF, interactions and local policy. Seek specialist advice for age 16–17. No medicine or dose is selected here.

Do not start an analgesic ladder for primary pain

For chronic primary pain, NG193 says not to initiate opioids, gabapentinoids, NSAIDs or paracetamol, among other listed medicines. This is indication-specific, not a ban on treating a separate condition. Check the full exclusions; do not import pregabalin-first US/EULAR prescribing into this NICE pathway.

11

Review existing medicines safely

If already taking a medicine NG193 advises against starting, review benefit and harm together. Agree safe continuation if benefit and few harms, or supported reduction when appropriate. Discuss withdrawal; do not abruptly stop antidepressants or dependence-forming medicines. Track the agreed plan.

Refer for uncertainty, complexity or unmet need

Seek relevant specialist advice when diagnosis is uncertain, findings suggest inflammatory/neurological disease, complexity exceeds skills, or pain/distress remains poorly controlled despite primary care. Clear fibromyalgia alone does not mandate rheumatology. Match the referral to the clinical question and local criteria.

Follow up and reassess changes

Agree who reviews function, symptoms, mood, treatment benefit/harms, referrals and new concerns. Reassess a flare or changed presentation; investigate new symptoms as indicated rather than assuming every problem is fibromyalgia. Offer suitable patient information and peer support, not as a replacement for clinical care.
