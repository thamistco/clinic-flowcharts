    Diagnosis and monitoring safeguards (NG136):

    ASSESS

    +

    Key NG136 recommendations (close paraphrase): 1.2.1 measure blood pressure in both arms when considering a diagnosis; if the difference between arms is more than 15 mmHg repeat the measurements, and if it persists use the arm with the higher reading thereafter. 1.2.10 if hypertension is not diagnosed, measure clinic blood pressure at least every 5 years, more often if raised but below 140/90. 1.2.11 [2026]: offer advice on healthy living, in line with the NHS information on healthy living, to people who have raised blood pressure but have not been diagnosed with hypertension. 1.3.1 use a formal CVD-risk estimation (the 1.4.10 10-year-risk >=10% threshold depends on this risk-estimation step). 1.4.17 advise people who want to self-monitor to use HBPM. 1.4.18 consider ABPM or HBPM in addition to clinic measurements for white-coat effect or masked hypertension; the corresponding ABPM/HBPM measurements are 5 mmHg lower than clinic measurements. 1.4.19 for HBPM users, provide training and advice, including what to do if target is not achieved. 1.4.28 offer people with isolated systolic hypertension (systolic 160 mmHg or more) the same treatment as people with both raised systolic and diastolic blood pressure. Also see NG136 1.2.9, 1.2.12, 1.2.13, 1.3.2, 1.4.15, 1.4.23, 1.4.25-1.4.27 and 1.1.1/1.1.3/1.1.4/1.1.7/1.1.8; open the current NG136 text for the complete list.

    2

    Check the same-day referral criteria

    URGENTREFER

    +

    This is clinician-led navigation, not an automated triage or emergency assessment. Do not continue routine treatment when same-day referral is indicated. SAME-DAY SPECIALIST ASSESSMENT (NG136 1.5.2): clinic blood pressure of 180/120 mmHg and higher with signs of retinal haemorrhage or papilloedema (accelerated hypertension), or life-threatening symptoms such as new onset confusion, chest pain, signs of heart failure, or acute kidney injury. SAME-DAY (NG136 1.5.3): suspected phaeochromocytoma (for example, labile or postural hypotension, headache, palpitations, pallor, abdominal pain or diaphoresis). Severe hypertension (180/120 mmHg or higher) WITHOUT those features (NG136 1.5.1, close paraphrase): investigate for target organ damage as soon as possible; if target organ damage is identified, consider starting antihypertensive treatment immediately without waiting for ABPM/HBPM; if none, repeat clinic BP within 7 days or arrange ABPM (or HBPM) with a clinical review within 7 days. Covers NG136 recommendations: 1.5.2, 1.5.3, 1.5.1.

    Blood pressure stage definitions

    ASSESS

    +

    Stage 1: clinic 140/90 mmHg to 159/99 mmHg and ABPM daytime average or HBPM 135/85 mmHg to 149/94 mmHg. Stage 2: clinic 160/100 mmHg or higher but less than 180/120 mmHg and ABPM daytime average or HBPM 150/95 mmHg or higher. Stage 3 (severe): clinic systolic 180 mmHg or higher, or clinic diastolic 120 mmHg or higher (NG136 Terms used).

    Assess risk, organ damage and context

Postural hypotension (NG136 1.1.5/1.1.6): in people with symptoms including falls or postural dizziness, measure BP lying (or seated if inconvenient) and again after standing for at least 1 minute; if systolic falls by 20 mmHg or more, or diastolic by 10 mmHg or more, consider likely causes including reviewing current medication, and manage accordingly (close paraphrase).

    7

    ACE inhibitor or ARB: retain the safeguards

    DRUG

    +

    Covers NG136 recommendations: 1.4.32, 1.4.30, 1.4.33, 1.4.34. Monitoring schedule (CKS hypertension scenario): check renal function and electrolytes before starting, 1–2 weeks after starting and after each dose increase, and regularly thereafter. Action thresholds (CKS CKD section): eGFR fall <25% or creatinine rise <30% – do not modify, retest in 1–2 weeks; eGFR fall >=25% or creatinine rise >=30% – investigate other causes and stop/reduce if none found; non-CKD creatinine rise >50% – follow local guidelines. A CKS-vs-SPS/NG106 threshold-framing difference is UNRESOLVED, not decided here – check both sources. MHRA Drug Safety Update (published 16 June 2026): ACE-inhibitor angioedema can be delayed-onset weeks to years into use; bradykinin-mediated angioedema may not respond to standard anaphylaxis treatment. Pregnancy: ACE inhibitors/ARBs are not for use in pregnancy – ask about pregnancy intention and stop ideally as soon as pregnancy is detected (CKS corroborated).

    •

    Drug-card safeguards: losartan, indapamide, amlodipine, ramipril

    DRUG

    +

    LOSARTAN: no initial dosage adjustment is necessary in renal impairment or haemodialysis; in patients over 75 years, consideration should be given to initiating with 25 mg; dosage adjustment is not usually necessary for the elderly (SmPC, emc 101967). Not confirmed against the BNF directly (possible BNF-vs-SmPC framing difference – follow the dispensed product's SmPC and record which guided the start). INDAPAMIDE: in severe renal failure (creatinine clearance below 30 ml/min) treatment is CONTRAINDICATED, as is severe hepatic impairment; also contraindicated in hypersensitivity to the active substance or to other sulfonamides (SmPC, emc 14345). AMLODIPINE: in hepatic impairment, initiate at low dose and titrate slowly (BNF). RAMIPRIL in pregnancy: the sources agree in direction – not for use in pregnancy (BNF Pregnancy section: avoid; MHRA/NG136: not for use in pregnancy). First dose preferably at bedtime (first-dose hypotension, BNF).

    Resistant hypertension: source-only boundary

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    This pilot stops before step-4 prescribing. Step-4 gates from the full source (NG136 1.4.49-1.4.52): consider spironolactone only if blood potassium is 4.5 mmol/L or lower, with caution in reduced eGFR; if potassium is above 4.5 mmol/L, consider an alpha-blocker or beta-blocker; monitor sodium, potassium and renal function within 1 month of starting and repeat as needed; seek specialist advice if BP remains uncontrolled on 4 drugs. No spironolactone, alpha-blocker or beta-blocker dose has been transcribed here. Open the full step-4 source, including potassium, renal function and monitoring requirements, before acting.

    Targets and review are part of the pathway

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Clinic targets (NG136 1.4.20/1.4.21): aged under 80 – reduce clinic blood pressure to below 140/90 mmHg and maintain it; aged 80 and over – below 150/90 mmHg, using clinical judgement for people with frailty or multimorbidity. ABPM and HBPM equivalents are 5 mmHg lower than clinic measurements (1.4.18). CKD and type 1 diabetes targets are not covered by this pilot – see NG136 tables 1 and 2 and NG203 (the October 2026 NG136 amendment clarifies when NG203 applies). Check their condition-specific guidance. Dose titration/monitoring remains medicine- and patient-specific. Covers NG136 recommendations: 1.4.16, 1.4.20, 1.4.21, 1.4.22, 1.4.24.

    Alternative (non-UK) guidance – for comparison only

    Not UK guidance. Shown because newer non-UK guidance or trials report better outcomes for this problem. Do not use it in place of the UK chart unless a local specialist pathway adopts it. AI-assisted review, not clinical sign-off.

    What differs: ESC 2024 recommends a systolic target of 120–129 mmHg for most adults on BP-lowering treatment, provided it is tolerated. NICE NG136 targets clinic BP below 140/90 mmHg under 80 and below 150/90 mmHg at 80 and over. ESC also adds an 'elevated BP' category (office 120–139/70–89 mmHg) and recommends drug treatment for elevated BP when 10-year CVD risk is 10% or more and BP stays at 130/80 mmHg or higher after 3 months of lifestyle measures.

    Evidence for better outcomes: SPRINT (NEJM 2015; high-risk adults without diabetes; target under 120 vs under 140 mmHg): primary composite HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.64–0.89); all-cause mortality HR 0.73 (0.60–0.90). Hypotension, syncope, electrolyte abnormalities and AKI were more common, but injurious falls were not. ESPRIT (Lancet 2024; 11,255 high-risk adults with or without diabetes or previous stroke): major vascular events HR 0.88 (0.78–0.99), with more serious syncope (0.4% vs 0.1%).

    UK licensing and availability: No new medicines: the same UK-licensed drug classes are used; local formulary choice applies.

    1

    Classify office BP (ESC 2024)

    ASSESS

    +

    Elevated BP: office systolic 120–139 mmHg or diastolic 70–89 mmHg. Hypertension: office systolic 140 mmHg or higher, or diastolic 90 mmHg or higher.

    2

    Elevated BP and high CVD risk

    SUPPORT

    +

    Lifestyle measures for 3 months first. Drug treatment is then recommended if 10-year CVD risk is 10% or more and confirmed BP is 130/80 mmHg or higher.

    3

    Hypertension: lifestyle plus drugs

    DRUG

    +

    Lifestyle measures plus BP-lowering drug treatment; choice of initial combination per the full ESC guideline (not re-verified in this review).

    4

    Target 120–129 mmHg systolic if tolerated

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Aim for 120–129 mmHg systolic in adults on BP-lowering treatment. If not tolerated, use a more relaxed target; in selected people, as low as reasonably achievable. People under 85 who are not moderately to severely frail follow the same approach as younger adults if treatment is well tolerated.

    Watch for harms of intensive control

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Check for orthostatic symptoms, syncope, electrolyte changes and AKI after each step up (SPRINT, ESPRIT).
