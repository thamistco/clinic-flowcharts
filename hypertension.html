Diagnosis and monitoring safeguards (NG136):

ASSESS

+

Key NG136 recommendations (close paraphrase): 1.2.1 measure blood pressure in both arms when considering a diagnosis; if the difference between arms is more than 15 mmHg repeat the measurements, and if it persists use the arm with the higher reading thereafter. 1.2.10 if hypertension is not diagnosed, measure clinic blood pressure at least every 5 years, more often if raised but below 140/90. 1.3.1 use a formal CVD-risk estimation (the 1.4.10 10-year-risk >=10% threshold depends on this risk-estimation step). 1.4.17 advise people who want to self-monitor to use HBPM. 1.4.18 consider ABPM or HBPM in addition to clinic measurements for white-coat effect or masked hypertension; the corresponding ABPM/HBPM measurements are 5 mmHg lower than clinic measurements. 1.4.19 for HBPM users, provide training and advice, including what to do if target is not achieved. 1.4.28 offer people with isolated systolic hypertension (systolic 160 mmHg or more) the same treatment as people with both raised systolic and diastolic blood pressure. Also see NG136 1.2.9, 1.2.12, 1.2.13, 1.3.2, 1.4.15, 1.4.23, 1.4.25-1.4.27 and 1.1.1/1.1.3/1.1.4/1.1.7/1.1.8; open the current NG136 text for the complete list.

Blood pressure stage definitions

ASSESS

+

Stage 1: clinic 140/90 mmHg to 159/99 mmHg and ABPM daytime average or HBPM 135/85 mmHg to 149/94 mmHg. Stage 2: clinic 160/100 mmHg or higher but less than 180/120 mmHg and ABPM daytime average or HBPM 150/95 mmHg or higher. Stage 3 (severe): clinic systolic 180 mmHg or higher, or clinic diastolic 120 mmHg or higher (NG136 Terms used).

7

ACE inhibitor or ARB: retain the safeguards

DRUG

+

Covers NG136 recommendations: 1.4.32, 1.4.30, 1.4.33, 1.4.34. Monitoring schedule (CKS hypertension scenario): check renal function and electrolytes before starting, 1-2 weeks after starting and after each dose increase, and regularly thereafter. Action thresholds (CKS CKD section): eGFR fall <25% or creatinine rise <30% - do not modify, retest in 1-2 weeks; eGFR fall >=25% or creatinine rise >=30% - investigate other causes and stop/reduce if none found; non-CKD creatinine rise >50% - follow local guidelines. A CKS-vs-SPS/NG106 threshold-framing difference is UNRESOLVED, not decided here - check both sources. MHRA Drug Safety Update (published 16 June 2026): ACE-inhibitor angioedema can be delayed-onset weeks to years into use; bradykinin-mediated angioedema may not respond to standard anaphylaxis treatment. Pregnancy: ACE inhibitors/ARBs are not for use in pregnancy - ask about pregnancy intention and stop ideally as soon as pregnancy is detected (CKS corroborated).

Drug-card safeguards: losartan, indapamide, amlodipine, ramipril

DRUG

+

LOSARTAN: no initial dosage adjustment is necessary in renal impairment or haemodialysis; in patients over 75 years, consideration should be given to initiating with 25 mg; dosage adjustment is not usually necessary for the elderly (SmPC, emc 101967). Not confirmed against the BNF directly (possible BNF-vs-SmPC framing difference - follow the dispensed product's SmPC and record which guided the start). INDAPAMIDE: in severe renal failure (creatinine clearance below 30 ml/min) treatment is CONTRAINDICATED, as is severe hepatic impairment; also contraindicated in hypersensitivity to the active substance or to other sulfonamides (SmPC, emc 14345). AMLODIPINE: in hepatic impairment, initiate at low dose and titrate slowly (BNF). RAMIPRIL in pregnancy: the sources agree in direction - not for use in pregnancy (BNF Pregnancy section: avoid; MHRA/NG136: not for use in pregnancy). First dose preferably at bedtime (first-dose hypotension, BNF).

Lifestyle measures

SUPPORT

+

drug-safety limb from NG136 1.4.5 (verbatim): encourage people to keep dietary sodium intake low, by reducing or substituting sodium salt; note that salt substitutes containing potassium chloride should not be used by older people, people with diabetes, pregnant women, people with kidney disease and people taking some antihypertensive drugs, such as ACE inhibitors and angiotensin II receptor blockers. The wider NG136 1.4.1-1.4.8 lifestyle set (healthy diet, exercise, alcohol, caffeine) is not listed in full here; open the current NG136 section 1.4 rather than relying on this summary.

Resistant hypertension: source-only boundary

REVIEW/LIMIT

+

This pilot stops before step-4 prescribing. Step-4 gates from the full source (NG136 1.4.49-1.4.52): consider spironolactone only if blood potassium is 4.5 mmol/L or lower, with caution in reduced eGFR; if potassium is above 4.5 mmol/L, consider an alpha-blocker or beta-blocker; monitor sodium, potassium and renal function within 1 month of starting and repeat as needed; seek specialist advice if BP remains uncontrolled on 4 drugs. No spironolactone, alpha-blocker or beta-blocker dose has been transcribed here. Open the full step-4 source, including potassium, renal function and monitoring requirements, before acting.
