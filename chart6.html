    1

    Triage infection, crusting and vulnerability

    URGENT

    +

    Sepsis and severe illness first. Suspected sepsis: emergency assessment (NHS sepsis guidance: get immediate medical help, call 999 or go to A&E). Systemically unwell: assess for sepsis - treat as an emergency if suspected; get immediate medical help when seriously unwell even without every symptom (NHS: trust your instincts). Secondary bacterial infection in someone not systemically unwell and without suspected crusted scabies: non-urgent primary-care assessment (NHS advice level). Suspected crusted scabies or infant under 2 months: specialist advice. Immunosuppression needs an individual assessment. Direction-supported limb: the immunosuppression point is supported in direction by CKS/BASHH; the source defines no specific action and none is added here.

    2

    Assess the patient, not just a rash

    ASSESS

    +

    Ask about night-time itch, onset, previous episodes, close contacts and treatments already used. Examine typical sites and look for burrows. Check the whole clinical picture, including eczema, bites and other causes; absent itch does not exclude scabies.

    3

    Make a household and close-contact plan

    ASSESS

    +

    Include everyone living in the home, even without symptoms: everyone in the home needs to be treated at the same time. Anyone with sexual contact in the past 8 weeks should also be treated. Identify other prolonged or frequent skin-to-skin contacts, including carers or visiting family where relevant. Symptoms can be delayed. Do not assume every casual contact needs treatment.

    4

    Choose suitable treatment for each person

    DRUG

    +

    CKS lists permethrin 5% cream or oral ivermectin as first-line options for classical scabies. LOCAL (Sussex Formulary 13.2.3, accessed 21 November 2026): 'Permethrin is the preferred agent except where there is an allergy to any of its constituents'; ivermectin is '3rd line for scabies if topical treatments have failed or are unavailable'. Local formulary choice applies for Sussex prescribing; status: check colour on the live page. Choice depends on age/weight, pregnancy or breastfeeding, preference and the household plan. Ivermectin second-dose conflict (presented, not resolved): the product licence (ivermectin 3 mg tablets SmPC, emc 100011) and BNF do NOT recommend a routine second dose for classical scabies - a second dose within 2 weeks of the initial dose should only be considered when new specific lesions occur or the parasitologic examination is positive at that date, and persistence of pruritus or scraping lesions does not justify a second treatment before 4 weeks (recovery is definite only after 4 weeks). CKS (September 2025) and UKHSA (updated 29 April 2026, recommend two doses one week apart; BASHH 2025 (UK national guideline on the management of scabies, Morris et al) recommends a second dose 7-14 days after the first for either first-line treatment (ivermectin: day 8, up to day 15 if necessary). The prescriber reconciles and documents the chosen course; this chart withholds a routine second-dose instruction. Crusted scabies differs: a repeat dose within 8 to 15 days may be necessary (SmPC). No oral dose is given in this chart.

    5

    Permethrin: cover the skin properly

    DRUG

    +

    For a clinician-selected permethrin regimen, apply 5% cream to cool, dry skin over the whole body, not only itchy areas. Head/neck coverage differs by source and age (presented, not resolved): BNF/guideline advice includes face, scalp, neck and ears; the permethrin 5% cream SmPC (emc 15508) says adults, adolescents and children over 2 years apply to the whole body including neck, palms and soles, and the head and face can be spared unless scabies efflorescences are present there; children under 2 years apply to the whole body including face, ears and scalp; elderly patients over 65 should additionally treat face, ears and scalp. State which instruction the patient should follow for the actual preparation supplied; explain the clinician's instructions and do not silently substitute a different product. The example product SmPC date of revision of the text is 20/06/2025. Take care not to allow the cream to get into the eyes or to come into contact with mucous membranes (for example the nasopharyngeal space or genital area) or open wounds (Permethrin 5% w/w Dermal Cream SmPC section 4.4, emc product 15508).

    6

    Complete the timed treatment course

    DRUG

    +

    BNF permethrin regimen: leave on for 8-12 hours, wash off, and repeat after 7 days. BASHH 2025) advises leaving the cream on for 12 hours, then washing it off, and reapplying to hands or other areas washed within the 12 hours. Replace cream washed off during the prescribed contact time. Supply enough for both complete applications for every treated person.

    7

    Babies, pregnancy and breastfeeding

    SUPPORT

    +

    Children under 2 years need GP assessment, not unsupervised pharmacy treatment. Under 2 months: seek specialist advice. In breastfeeding, avoid infant contact with treated skin; BASHH 2025, GPP) advises removing the cream or liquid from the nipples before breast/chest-feeding and reapplying treatment afterwards. The product position is more cautious (permethrin SmPC 4.6,: it is not known whether permethrin is excreted in human breast milk, concentrations are unlikely to present any risk to the infant, and consideration should be given to withholding treatment during nursing or temporarily discontinuing nursing. The two positions point in different directions - reconcile with current specialist/pharmacy advice for the actual preparation. Do not automatically stop breastfeeding or choose oral ivermectin for the whole household.

    8

    Coordinate people, dates and practical help

    SUPPORT

    +

    Treat the patient and identified household/close contacts together, including asymptomatic contacts. Confirm access, enough treatment, application help, laundry and the repeat date. Do not leave a diagnosed patient untreated while a wider outbreak response is organised.

    9

    Clothing, bedding and towels

    SUPPORT

    +

    Coordinate decontamination with treatment. Wash bedding, clothing and towels at 60°C or above; use a hot dryer where suitable. Seal items that cannot be washed in a bag for at least 72 hours. Avoid sharing these items.

    10

    Close contact, sex, school and work

    SUPPORT

    +

    NHS advice: avoid sex and close physical contact until the full treatment course is completed. Age 5+ may return to school/work once treatment starts, avoiding close contact for 24 hours; under-5s return to nursery/pre-school 24 hours after the first treatment.

    11

    Communal setting or repeated linked cases

    KEY

    +

    A care home, hostel or other residential setting needs its own infection-control and contact-tracing plan. UKHSA (updated 29 April 2026) defines an outbreak as 2 or more epidemiologically linked cases of scabies within an 8-week period within a setting; 2 or more cases within 8 weeks with no epidemiological link identified is a cluster, and clusters become outbreaks if a link is made. Seek UKHSA/health-protection-team advice for outbreak management.

    12

    Review itch, new lesions and reinfestation

    REVIEW/LIMIT

    +

    Itch can persist for several weeks and does not alone prove live mites or resistance. Reassess if it worsens, new burrows appear, infection develops, or itch persists 2–4 weeks after the final treatment. Check diagnosis, application, repeat dose and untreated contacts before more treatment.
