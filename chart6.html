Scabies: patient and household

Recognise classical scabies, choose individual treatment and coordinate household/close contacts. Infants, pregnancy, crusted disease and communal outbreaks have different needs. Generic clinical reference, not a diagnosis or a family prescription.

Source-checked review copy, not clinically validated.

1

Triage infection, crusting and vulnerability

Systemic illness or suspected sepsis needs emergency assessment. Secondary bacterial infection needs same-day assessment. Suspected crusted scabies, immunosuppression or an infant under 2 months needs specialist input, not this routine pathway.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Crusted scabies may itch little or not at all and can be very infectious. Arrange prompt specialist advice and appropriate infection prevention; hospital admission may be required. Assess skin breaks, pain, spreading inflammation, fever and general condition. Treat complications through their own pathway. Do not apply permethrin to broken or secondarily infected skin without resolving the treatment plan.

  • NICE CKS: recognition and diagnosis
  • NHS England October 2026 pharmacy prescribing pathway: narrower scope
2

Assess the patient, not just a rash

Ask about night-time itch, onset, previous episodes, close contacts and treatments already used. Examine typical sites and look for burrows. Check the whole clinical picture, including eczema, bites and other causes; absent itch does not exclude scabies.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Consider wrists, finger webs, waist, axillae, buttocks and genital skin, with consent and a chaperone as appropriate. Infants and older people may have head, palm or sole involvement. Colour changes can be less obvious on brown/black skin. Seek experienced assessment or appropriate dermoscopy/microscopy if uncertain; a negative scraping does not reliably exclude infestation.

  • NICE CKS: recognition and diagnosis
  • BASHH UK scabies guideline 2025, age 13+ sexual-health scope
3

Make a household and close-contact plan

Include everyone living in the home, even without symptoms. Identify sexual partners and other prolonged or frequent skin-to-skin contacts, including carers or visiting family where relevant. Symptoms can be delayed. Do not assume every casual contact needs treatment.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Record contact categories and arrange confidential notification and assessment, not relatives' personal details on this website. Look-back wording differs: NHS says sexual contacts in the past 8 weeks; CKS says past month; BASHH traces sexual/household/close contacts from 2 months before symptom onset. Use the full relevant guideline and sexual-health support rather than silently reducing the window. Household members require treatment regardless of symptoms.

  • NICE CKS: management, revised September 2025
  • NHS: scabies, household and practical advice
  • BASHH UK scabies guideline 2025, age 13+ sexual-health scope
4

Choose suitable treatment for each person

CKS lists permethrin 5% cream or oral ivermectin as first-line options for classical scabies. Choice depends on age/weight, pregnancy or breastfeeding, skin disease, allergy, medicines and ability to apply treatment. Family members do not automatically share one regimen.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Permethrin cream is not the head-lice rinse. Oral ivermectin is not the topical rosacea preparation. Do not prescribe oral ivermectin below 15 kg; pregnancy, breastfeeding, hepatic disease, interactions and high body weight need current prescribing checks. No tablet calculator or oral dose is provided here because CKS/BASHH repeat-dose advice differs from the live BNF/product wording. See the source-limits panel.

  • NICE CKS: management, revised September 2025
  • BNF: permethrin, scabies indication
  • CKS: oral ivermectin prescribing and restrictions
  • BNF: ivermectin, oral scabies indication
5

Permethrin: cover the skin properly

For a clinician-selected permethrin regimen, apply 5% cream to cool, dry skin over the whole body, not only itchy areas. BNF includes face, scalp, neck and ears. Avoid eyes, mouth, mucosa and open wounds; give age-appropriate application advice.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Pay attention to finger/toe webs, under trimmed nails, wrists, skin folds, soles, buttocks and external genital skin, avoiding mucosa. Use help for hard-to-reach areas. Keep young children from licking treated hands/skin; avoid the mouth area. Guideline head/neck advice is broader than some product leaflets: explain the clinician's instructions, do not silently substitute a different product.

  • BNF: permethrin, scabies indication
  • BASHH UK scabies guideline 2025, age 13+ sexual-health scope
  • Permethrin 5% cream: example product SmPC, updated 30 June 2025
6

Complete the timed treatment course

BNF permethrin regimen: leave on for 8–12 hours, wash off, and repeat after 7 days. BASHH uses 12 hours. Replace cream washed off during the prescribed contact time. Supply enough for both complete applications for every treated person.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Most adults need about one 30 g tube per application, some up to two; children need age/body-surface-specific quantities and supervision. These are not a universal family prescription. Write the chosen contact time and both application dates clearly. Fabrics exposed to the cream burn more easily: no smoking or naked flames. Follow product-specific excipient/latex and allergy advice.

  • BNF: permethrin, scabies indication
  • BASHH UK scabies guideline 2025, age 13+ sexual-health scope
  • Permethrin 5% cream: example product SmPC, updated 30 June 2025
7

Babies, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Children under 2 years need GP assessment, not unsupervised pharmacy treatment. Under 2 months: seek specialist advice. Permethrin at 2–23 months needs close medical supervision. Pregnancy/breastfeeding require an individual treatment plan, usually topical permethrin under BASHH advice.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Do not extrapolate the adult BASHH guideline to infant dosing. In breastfeeding, avoid infant contact with treated skin; BASHH advises removing treatment from nipples before feeding and reapplying afterwards. Product lactation wording is more cautious, so reconcile with current specialist/pharmacy advice for the actual preparation. Do not automatically stop breastfeeding or choose oral ivermectin for the whole household.

8

Coordinate people, dates and practical help

Treat the patient and identified household/close contacts together, including asymptomatic contacts. Confirm access, enough treatment, application help, laundry and the repeat date. Do not leave a diagnosed patient untreated while a wider outbreak response is organised.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Each contact needs a suitable assessment and supply route. If coordination fails, seek help rather than assuming treatment has happened; record actual planned/completed status in the clinical record. For communal settings, UKHSA says treat diagnosed cases promptly and arrange wider coordinated treatment; the case may need re-treatment if contacts were delayed.

  • NICE CKS: management, revised September 2025
  • NHS: scabies, household and practical advice
  • BASHH UK scabies guideline 2025, age 13+ sexual-health scope
  • UKHSA communal-residential cases/outbreaks, updated 29 April 2026
9

Clothing, bedding and towels

Coordinate decontamination with treatment. Wash bedding, clothing and towels at 60°C or above; use a hot dryer where suitable. Seal items that cannot be washed in a bag for at least 72 hours. Avoid sharing these items.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Follow fabric safety instructions and plan practical support where laundering is difficult. This is household advice for classical scabies, not a complete crusted-scabies or care-home cleaning protocol. Avoid unnecessary repeated scabicides or harsh chemical cleaning of skin. Use the setting-specific UKHSA plan for outbreaks.

  • NICE CKS: management, revised September 2025
  • NHS: scabies, household and practical advice
10

Close contact, sex, school and work

NHS advice: avoid sex and close physical contact until the full treatment course is completed. Age 5+ may return to school/work once treatment starts, avoiding close contact for 24 hours; under-5s return to nursery/pre-school 24 hours after the first treatment.

+

Source scope and exceptions

These are not the same as clearance for hands-on care or communal settings. Follow occupational/setting infection-control advice. NHS public advice is more cautious about sex/close contact than BASHH's minimum interval after the first completed treatment; this guide follows the NHS public-facing advice, not a single universal clearance time. Consider sexual-health referral for STI assessment and partner notification where relevant.

  • NHS: scabies, household and practical advice
  • BASHH UK scabies guideline 2025, age 13+ sexual-health scope
  • UKHSA communal-residential cases/outbreaks, updated 29 April 2026
11

Communal setting or repeated linked cases

A care home, hostel or other residential setting needs its own infection-control and contact-tracing plan. UKHSA defines an outbreak as 2 or more cases at the same setting within 8 weeks. Do not treat a wider setting as just one household.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Use the current UKHSA setting guidance for assessment, contacts, PPE, cleaning and transfers. BASHH 2025 advises HPT contact for 2 or more linked closed-setting cases in 8 weeks; the newer UKHSA setting guidance does not require routine reporting. Scabies is not notifiable and UKHSA does not require routine HPT reporting of every case/outbreak. Seek local health protection support if the setting struggles despite following the guidance; use clinical specialist support for crusted or difficult disease. No local service acceptance or supply is promised.

  • UKHSA communal-residential cases/outbreaks, updated 29 April 2026
12

Review itch, new lesions and reinfestation

Itch can persist for several weeks and does not alone prove live mites or resistance. Reassess if it worsens, new burrows appear, infection develops, or itch persists 2–4 weeks after the final treatment. Check diagnosis, application, repeat dose and untreated contacts before more treatment.

+

Source scope and exceptions

Offer appropriate itch/eczema care after assessment; do not automatically repeat scabicides or add steroid/sedating-antihistamine prescriptions for everyone. Persistent nodules or confirmed treatment failure may need dermatology. Record the owner, review date and escalation advice. BASHH notes itch sometimes lasts longer despite eradication; this is a reason to assess, not to ignore new lesions or self-treat repeatedly.

Sources, dates and limits

Differences that affect use

  • Contact look-back: NHS uses sexual contact in the past 8 weeks; CKS says past month; BASHH traces contacts from 2 months before symptom onset. The communal-setting UKHSA window is 8 weeks before diagnosis. These are different source/population definitions, not interchangeable numbers.
  • Oral ivermectin: CKS September 2025 recommends a planned second dose after 7 days, and BASHH 2025 recommends two doses 7–14 days apart. The live BNF scabies entry says repeat within 2 weeks only for new specific lesions or positive parasitology. CKS also retains a later statement about waiting 4 weeks before further treatment for persistent itch. Distinguish a planned course from extra retreatment. No oral dose, weight-to-tablet table or food instruction is reproduced: the prescriber must reconcile the chosen guideline, product licence and current BNF/pharmacy advice.
  • Permethrin: BNF recommends whole-body coverage including head/neck and 8–12 hours, twice 7 days apart; BASHH uses 12 hours. Some product instructions spare the head/face in older children/adults. The example SmPC also contains differing repeat-treatment wording within its own text. Explain the chosen guideline-based directions and check the actual preparation. Do not use a head-lice rinse.
  • Age and access: permethrin licensing from 2 months is not permission for unsupervised infant treatment. NHS says under-2s see a GP; BNF/SmPC require medical supervision at 2–23 months; under-2-month treatment needs specialist advice. BASHH is primarily adult/age-13+ sexual-health guidance. The October 2026 NHS England pharmacy pathway has narrower eligibility and only permethrin; it is not the full GP treatment menu or a guarantee of local availability.
  • Pregnancy, breastfeeding and return to contact: BASHH and product/BNF advice differ in detail. Use a patient-specific plan. This page follows NHS public-facing avoidance/return advice, with separate setting-specific controls. No claim of universal clearance after one dose.
